# MKT-18 · How the industry writes qualities and risks

Surveyed on 2026-11-26, from the ISO catalogue pages of ISO/IEC 25010:2023 and practitioners' guides to risk
registers and RAID logs (University of Waterloo's PMO, Atlassian, Rework, The Digital Project Manager). The
standard and the PMBOK Guide were not opened in full: the nine characteristics are named from memory and
checked only against the count the ISO page gives.

## Product quality (ISO/IEC 25010:2023)

| Element | The industry says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The model | Nine characteristics, each split into subcharacteristics, for ICT and software products; the 2011 edition's quality in use moved to ISO/IEC 25002 | ISO, *ISO/IEC 25010:2023* |
| The nine | Functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, portability, and safety, new in 2023 | Same, by count; names not read |
| A requirement | A quality is stated as something measurable, not as an adjective | Same |

## Risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies

| Element | The industry says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| RAID log | Risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies in one living document, at moderate depth | Rework; The Digital Project Manager |
| A risk | ID, description, probability, impact, a score (probability by impact), an owner, a response and a status | Rework, *Risk register*; Atlassian |
| An assumption | What is taken as true, pending validation, with what happens if it is wrong | University of Waterloo PMO, *RAID log* |
| An issue | A risk that happened: an owner and a resolution | Same |
| When | Reviewed while the project runs, and at its close: which risks happened | Rework |
