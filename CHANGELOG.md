# Changelog

Versions follow [SemVer](https://semver.org). While the major is `0`, any minor version may change
the format; the spec of each version is the tag `v<version>`.

## Unreleased

- The same discovery for every client, before any PRD (DEC-30, CHG-038, `@openpedd/cli` 0.10.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.12.0): a business use case may list `Includes` (what it always runs) and `Extends` (`BUC-nn · when <condition>`), and a step naming a process of another business use case includes it; `readBusinessMap` gives them as `includes` and `extends`, and `use-cases` draws the `«include»` and `«extend»` from the map alone, a missing end loose (FR-VEW-23). The as-is adds `Documents` and `Rules and regulations`, read as `documents` and `rules`. Industry glossaries live in `industries/`, researched once; a `Glossary` name defined by a web link is not loose (FR-VEW-24). The first: property insurance in Argentina, for buildings.
- Activity diagrams as RUP draws a business use case (CHG-037, `@openpedd/cli` 0.9.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.11.0): a process step may name, in `Object`, the business entity it leaves in a state (`ENT-nn · <state>`, several separated by `;`); the `activity` view draws it as an object node `Name [state]` with an object flow, and each business use case as an overview of its processes, each linked to its diagram (FR-VEW-22). An `Object` naming a missing entity, or a state it lacks, is loose. The map of a definition (ARCH-24) is linked from the README, the spec's README, AGENTS.md and the template.
- Business use cases and the map of a definition (CHG-037, `@openpedd/cli` 0.8.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.10.0): the business map's as-is lists its business use cases (`BUC` · `Business use case` · `Kind` `core`/`supporting`/`management` · `Business actor` · `Processes`), as UML and RUP define them (MKT-16); `readBusinessMap` gives them as `useCases`; the `use-cases` view draws them inside the business's boundary with their business actors, and `business` links each to the processes that realize it (FR-VEW-21); a process no business use case holds and a `core` one with no business actor are loose. ARCH-24 maps every level of a definition, from the business context to the data model, in order.
- Foundations first, PRDs one at a time (RULE-30, CHG-037): a business map lists the later modules too, and its foundations hold them; each PRD is written when its module is next. A module the index lists as `unmapped` exists for `pedd check`, and an entity another document cites is not loose while modules wait for their PRDs (FR-VEW-20); `readSpec` gives them as `unmapped`. `budgets` in `workspace.yaml` takes a folder or a file (`foundations/business-map/: 8000`, the default), the longest winning over its layer (RULE-04); the template's `check:size` agrees. The template README says to push `main` and `dev` first.
- `pedd records <change> pr|tasks|report|accept` (`@openpedd/cli` 0.7.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.9.0): the four counted checks that nothing went unrecorded (ARCH-11). `pr`: the PR's `## Spec` section (`Covers:`, `Assumptions:`, `Deviations:`, IDs or `none`) against the change's deltas and the task's own. `tasks`: every task of the checklist ticked, with its PR merged into the change branch. `report`: every delta in the Acceptance Report, heaviest first. `accept`: every delta decided. Each problem comes with its fix; `--json` for CI.
- Who answers a sweep (ARCH-16): the client the specify questions, the product engineer the architect and design ones, and the AI decides the plan's by their recommendation, recorded as its own and never asked. The sheet splits what each person answers; `pedd sweep <change> answer --as client|builder` takes only that person's (`@openpedd/cli` 0.5.0). `@openpedd/reader` 0.7.0 adds `applyAnswers` (answers already structured, as the Cloud sends them), `decideByAi` and `pendingFor`.
- `pedd sweep <change>` (`@openpedd/cli` 0.4.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.6.0): one finder per stage still ahead (from the PRDs' least advanced status), run at once as read-only sessions (`workspace.yaml` `agents.finder`, default `claude -p` with Read, Grep and Glob), merged under their owner, weighed by ARCH-23, the high asked and the rest shown decided, in `changes/<CHG>/sweep.md`. `pedd sweep <change> answer "all as recommended, except 3: b"` takes the one reply, says where each answer goes, and reopens what an answer changed; running the sweep again rewrites only those (round 2). A finder that fails is reported as not run. `--stages`, `--all`, `--ask-all`, `--accept-recommended`, `--from`, `--json`. The skill `/sweep` runs it from a session (`skills/sweep/SKILL.md`).
- `@openpedd/reader` 0.8.0, for apps that show the views: `@openpedd/reader/mount` exports `mountViews(root, data, options)`, the page's renderer as an ES module, with `links` per node, an `overlay` of live data by ID, a `diff` to mark, `labels` in the person's language, and `hash: false` with `onNavigate` for a host that keeps its own URL; `@openpedd/reader/views.css` its styles; `diffViews(older, newer)` (also `@openpedd/reader/diff`) gives per kind the nodes added, changed and removed and the edges, and refuses two formats. A view with no `loose` draws no counts. (FR-VEW-18, FR-VEW-19)
- `pedd view` draws fourteen views (`@openpedd/cli` 0.6.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.8.0): the `prd` view opens on modules and their PRDs, each PRD to its flows, use cases, transitions, requirements and criteria; four views of the business: `org` (the reporting tree with span of control), `activity` (one per process: one initial node, actions in each role's lane, decisions, a final per ending), `use-cases` (actors, a boundary per module, `«include»` and `«extend»`, exported as Mermaid `usecase-beta`) and `glossary`. An ID with a reference's prefix (`openpedd:ENT-20`) is the reference's, never loose; criteria written `AC-<MOD>-nn` count. (FR-VEW-10 to 17)
- Every kind of model is defined as the industry defines it, surveyed first (RULE-29, MKT-14, MKT-15): an entity as the ER model has it, with its kind (strong, weak, associative), its key and the cardinality of each relationship; transitions as UML's `trigger [guard]`; a step naming another flow includes it, an alternate extends it.
- `pedd weigh <change>` (`@openpedd/cli` 0.3.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.5.0 with `weigh` and `weighChange`): each assumption and deviation of a change weighed high, medium or low from the ARCH-23 table, with the words that set Undo, Reach and Evidence, Fan-out from `--fan-out`, the hard triggers, those with the same target and reason grouped, heaviest first; a missing `revert`, `evidence` or `if_no` is named. `--json` for a report. How each factor is read is in ARCH-23.
- Each decision the AI takes alone (a sweep question, an assumption, a deviation) gets a weight, high, medium or low, from a fixed table led by how hard it is to undo, then what it touches, what backs it and what builds on it; permissions, billing, personal data and security are always high. `pedd` computes it; the agent writes the evidence and what changes if the answer is no (RULE-28, ARCH-23, MKT-13).
- The sweep asks only the high; the medium and low appear in the same sheet as decided by their recommendation, and any can be overturned in the same reply. `--ask-all` asks them all.
- The `Acceptance Report` lists assumptions and deviations heaviest first; `accept` decides the high one by one and the low in one reply, and records the weight with each decision, so the table can be calibrated (Q-23).
- Nothing left unrecorded, counted by the tool: a task PR whose Spec section and deltas differ fails; `verify` starts only with every task ticked and merged; the report must hold every delta; `acceptance-merge` needs a decision on each. Assumptions found in the code with no delta are counted per task as undeclared.
- `@openpedd/reader` 0.4.0: the framework map's Skills view opens a card per skill, with the steps of its stage and every rule and mechanism it follows shown whole, with their tables and diagrams.
- `pedd view` (`@openpedd/cli` 0.2.0, `@openpedd/reader` 0.3.0): every project's spec drawn as ten maps, generated on each run, free (DEC-29): decisions, entities, states per entity, security, system context and containers, code, business, each PRD, traceability per requirement, and changes in flight. `.pedd/view/views.json` and one page with no network, laid out with ELK; `pedd view <kind> <id>` scopes it, `--format mermaid` prints it for a PR, `--watch` rebuilds it on each save. The reader adds `viewData`, `renderViews`, `toMermaid` and `scopeView` (ARCH-22), and `openpedd-reader views <root>`.
- `pedd check` fails on a loose item a change adds or touches, and lists the rest as pressure (RULE-27): an ID defined nowhere, a decision that cites nothing, an entity no PRD cites, a requirement no flow carries or no criterion proves, a transition, role or action the foundations lack, a table nothing protects, a to-be process with no mark. `--json` prints the checks. The template's CI runs `pedd` at `cli-v0.2.0`. `npm run view` now writes the framework map to `.pedd/map/`.
- The branch check in CI: `.github/workflows/pedd.yml`, here and in `template/`, runs `pedd branch check` first on every pull request (a branch misnamed, targeting the wrong branch or cut from another work branch fails, with the command that fixes it), then `pedd check`. The template runs `pedd` from this repository at the tag `cli-v0.1.0`; `workspace.yaml` in the template names the edit branch.
- `pedd` 0.1.0, in `packages/cli` (`@openpedd/cli`), on `@openpedd/reader`: `pedd check` runs the spec checks (frontmatter, links, size, a PRD's status against its requirements', history), and `npm run check` runs it; `pedd branch` cuts a change or a request from the base, numbered `CHG-nnn` with its `definition.md`, its design, architecture, task and edit branches from it, renames it at `spec-approval`, and `pedd branch check` is the CI branch check. - `@openpedd/reader` 0.2.0: `readSpec` reports what it cannot read in `problems` (a broken frontmatter, a history line that is not JSON) instead of reading around it, and gives the `Business Map` as `businessMap`, as-is and to-be, for the Cloud's business map screen; `openpedd-reader business-map` and `problems` from a shell. - `@openpedd/reader` 0.1.0, in `packages/reader`: `readSpec` (the model, with each requirement's status from its PRD's column or the PRD's), `readDefinition` · `writeDefinition`, `nextChangeId`, `mapData` and `renderMap`, and `openpedd-reader map <root>` for the map's data as JSON. `npm run view` runs on it, with the same output. Tagged `reader-v0.1.0`; not on npm yet (ARCH-20).
- A product starts as one monorepo: `spec/`, `apps/` (app, landing, docs, help) and `packages/` (`ui` with its Storybook, `i18n`), each app deployed on its own. Several repos only for a product adopted that way, or open-source code with a private spec (DEC-22).
- The code index: one generated file per module and package, regenerated on every merge to the base, that every agent reads first to know where to work (ARCH-19).
- A request is a change branch from the start, holding only its definition; dropped before `spec-approval`, the branch is deleted; after, it is retired through a change (DEC-23).
- `map` in business mode: a product engineer maps how a client's organization works today and will work with the system, the client approves it, and it feeds the foundations, the modules and the first roadmap (DEC-24).
- A product with screens and no `Brand System` gets a branding change before its first screens.
- `pedd branch` creates every work branch from the right base, read from the tracker; the CI action fails a PR cut from elsewhere, and agents are denied `git checkout -b` for work branches.
- Changes land in the order their requests were queued; the computed order applies only with several in flight or a hotfix, and a queued request gets the base merged in when it is taken.
- A versioned baseline of what every project starts with, each item required, recommended or if the project has it; a new practice enters as recommended; `pedd doctor` lists a built product's gaps and `pedd update` turns one into a small change. The workspace PRD is split: the baseline and the template live in `modules/workspace/baseline.md`.
- The core holds only the method's skills; others are extensions with their license checked, and what OpenPEDD hosts or pays for is a Cloud add-on (DEC-25).
- The project template, `template/`: one monorepo with the spec structure and each folder's guide, the apps and packages as empty skeletons, `workspace.yaml` with every default commented and `locales: [en]`, OpenPEDD's block for `AGENTS.md`, and `npm run check`. Start with `npx degit openpedd/openpedd/template`. `npm run check` here also checks that the template's scripts and guides match their sources. `branches` and `review` are now listed among the workspace fields.
- Requirement and PRD statuses renamed: `designing` is now `specified`, `ready` is now `designed`. The order: `draft`, `specified`, `designed`, `building`, `in_review`, `accepted`, `live`.
- Every product starts with i18n: English as the base, other languages added to `locales`; `init` writes `locales: [en]` and the language checks always run (DEC-21).
- What a new project comes with, in one table, and the project template (`template/`) that `pedd init` installs and the first client copies by hand.
- The first skill: `skills/specify-interview/SKILL.md`. Skills and judges live in `skills/` and `agents/`, each naming the spec documents it implements.
- `/specify-interview` and the minimum map: what a product, a module or a request needs to be defined, who answers each item (the client, a proposal, or the team), and the conversation that fills it (ARCH-18).
- A client defines a product in a conversation that runs until the definition is complete: questions computed first, two or three per turn, completeness counted per item, a summary to confirm (DEC-20, MKT-08).
- Each skill says what it reads and what it leaves, in its stage's table; every stage input is read by a skill and every output is left by one. `verify` declares the tests as an input: the Walkthrough draft comes from their steps.
- How a change is reviewed, in six layers: gates, project rules as lint, hunters per lens, a refuter, `pedd` deciding what blocks, and a person approving from the review signals. A finding confirmed in two changes becomes a rule.
- Default review lenses with their checklist, from the corrections of Reclee's PRs.
- The judges are part of the method: free, on every PR in the CI action, on the team's own subscription. The Cloud runs no judges.
- `pedd judges bench` and judge precision as a process metric.
- Small work is not delegated: a `small` task builds in one local session.
- `/build-findings`: fix the real findings, answer the rest, stop where the spec is wrong.
- Writing safely: `--dry-run`, backups, sensitive paths denied, the agent never installed. Personal settings that can only spend less than the team's.
- Skills through `npx skills add`, and a catalog of extensions by GitHub topic.
- `pedd sweep`: every open question of a change, from every stage ahead, asked at once with a recommendation each; `architect` in `guided` asks through it (RULE-26).
- Landing parallel changes: `pedd overlap` warns early, `pedd sync` keeps changes in flight close to the base, `pedd land` computes the order and lands them one at a time.
- A change's tasks are a checklist in the implementation sub-issue, not one issue each; task PRs link to it with `Part of`, which does not close it.
- `design` builds from the product's component catalog: each screen is listed as the pieces it is made of before drawing, a piece of its own says why none fits and enters the catalog with its story, the refactor pass reads copies across the whole code, and `design-judge` checks the catalog, the duplicates and axe in light and dark (MKT-06).
- Keeping agents' context small: one task per session, tests that report only failures, the pack pointing at the code, denied paths and a read hook; a task over 100,000 tokens is marked as badly cut.
- Where each stage runs (`local`, `cloud`, `self-hosted`), set in `workspace.yaml`; implementation in the cloud by default, one session per task, with a quota guard.
- At `spec-approval`, one tracker sub-issue per stage with a branch and a gate (architecture, design, implementation), with their blocks, unassigned and in the backlog; `plan` is the first item of the implementation checklist and the launch is the parent's last status.
- The tracker in its own document (ARCH-15): only `specify` creates issues, once, found again by their `Spec:` anchor and under the issue that already existed; statuses move from pull request events, never by hand; a definition of done ticked from CI; `/tracker-tidy` proposes the duplicates against the spec and the roadmap.

## 0.1.0 · 2026-11-11 · beta

The first versioned spec of the framework. Nothing to install yet.

- The cycle and its stages, from `map` and `specify` to `publish` and `measure`, with their judges.
- Tracks: `full`, `story`, `fix`, `hotfix`, `cover` and `chore`.
- Testing before code, the pipeline by moment, flags through OpenFeature, launching apart from deploying, and the Walkthrough.
- `pedd init` and `pedd adopt` for new and running products.
- Traceability with time per stage and step, and process metrics.
- What the framework can do, in one page: [`spec/capabilities.md`](spec/capabilities.md).
